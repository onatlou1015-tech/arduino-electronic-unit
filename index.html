Welcome to the Project

Over the next 10 weeks, you will become an engineer, a programmer, and a problem-solver. You will start with the basics of electronics and the Arduino microcontroller, then gradually build up your skills — one component at a time — until you have a fully working robot that can think, sense, move, and communicate.

This is not just a coding class. It is a journey through computational thinking — learning to break big problems into small pieces, spot patterns, ignore unnecessary details, and design step-by-step solutions. These are the same skills used by engineers at NASA, Tesla, and every robotics lab in the world.

By the end, you will present a working robot and explain how it could solve a real-world problem. Let's get building. 🤖

Lesson Introduction

What Is This Lesson About?

This unit introduces you to the world of microcontrollers and physical computing using the Arduino Uno. You will learn how to read electronic schematics, build working circuits on a breadboard, and write real C++ code that controls hardware — LEDs, motors, sensors, and Bluetooth modules.

Instead of learning each component in isolation, you will build up your skills step-by-step and then integrate everything into one complete project: a multi-functional robot that follows lines, avoids obstacles, and responds to commands from your phone.

Along the way, you will practise computational thinking — the same problem-solving approach used by professional engineers and software developers — by breaking big problems into small, manageable pieces and designing algorithms to solve them.

What You Will Be Able to Do at the End

By the end of this 10-week unit, you will confidently be able to:

  • Explain what a microcontroller is and how the Arduino Uno processes inputs and outputs.
  • Build safe, working circuits on a breadboard using LEDs, resistors, motors, and sensors.
  • Write and debug Arduino C++ sketches using setup(), loop(), conditionals, and functions.
  • Read sensor data — infrared, ultrasonic — and turn it into meaningful robot behaviour.
  • Control motors through an L298N driver and understand the H-bridge concept.
  • Communicate wirelessly via Bluetooth (HC-05/HC-06) and control your robot from a smartphone.
  • Apply computational thinking — decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithm design — to real engineering problems.
  • Present a working robot and explain how it solves a real-world problem in industries like healthcare, logistics, or warehousing.
What You Should Expect

Here's a quick look at the skills, tools and mindset you'll develop throughout the unit.

Hands-On Building

Wire circuits on breadboards, mount motors, connect sensors, and assemble a full robot chassis.

Real Programming

Write modular Arduino C++ code — functions, loops, conditionals — and debug it like a pro.

Problem Solving

Use computational thinking to break down complex tasks into manageable, testable steps.

Smartphone Control

Pair a Bluetooth module and control your robot wirelessly from a phone app you configure.

Testing & Iteration

Calibrate sensors, test behaviours, and improve your design through repeated trials.

Final Presentation

Demonstrate your robot and pitch a real-world application — from hospitals to warehouses.

Computational Thinking at the Core

Every week, you'll practise these four pillars — the same ones used by professional engineers.

Decomposition

Break the robot into power, sensing, movement, communication, and control subsystems.

Pattern Recognition

Spot repeated sensor behaviour — e.g., IR sees black → turn, sees white → go straight.

Abstraction

Treat components as "black boxes" — ultrasonic: trigger in → distance out.

Algorithm Design

Write step-by-step logic for line following, obstacle avoidance, and Bluetooth commands.

Your 10-Week Roadmap

Each week builds on the last — master one component before combining them all. Click on any topic below to open its dedicated lesson page.

Week
1–2
Introduction to Arduino & Basic Electronics

Microcontrollers, Arduino Uno pinout, digital vs analog pins, breadboards, resistors, LEDs.

Activity: Blink an LED (Hello World of Arduino)
Week 3
DC Motors & Motor Driver (L298P)

How DC motors work, why a motor driver is needed, and the H-bridge concept.

Activity: Spin two motors forward/backward
Week 4
IR Sensors (Line Following)

IR reflectance sensors, digital vs analog output, calibration on black vs white surfaces.

Activity: Detect black line and light an LED
Week 5
Ultrasonic Sensor (HC-SR04)

Sound waves and time-of-flight, Trigger/Echo pins, distance = (time × speed of sound) / 2.

Activity: Measure distance on Serial Monitor
Week 6
Bluetooth Module (HC-05 / HC-06)

Serial communication (TX/RX), pairing with a smartphone, sending and receiving characters.

Activity: Turn an LED on/off via phone app
Week 7
Chassis Assembly & Power

Mounting motors, wheels, castor, battery pack (9V/12V), voltage regulation, wiring diagram.

Activity: Assemble the robot chassis
Week
8–9
Integration & Programming

Combine all subsystems, write modular code (functions for each behaviour), debug and test.

Activity: Full robot integration & testing
Week 10
Presentation & Real-Life Connection

Demonstrate your robot and explain how it applies to real-world industries.

Activity: Final demo & pitch
